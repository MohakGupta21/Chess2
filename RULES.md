## Objective
Checkmate the opponent's king to win.
You cannot actually capture the king; the game ends when it is checkmated.
## Board & Setup
Chess is played on an 8×8 board (64 squares).
Each player starts with 16 pieces:
1 King
1 Queen
2 Rooks
2 Bishops
2 Knights
8 Pawns
White moves first, then players alternate turns.
## Piece Movement
Piece	Movement
♔ King	One square in any direction
♕ Queen	Any number of squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally
♖ Rook	Any number horizontally or vertically
♗ Bishop	Any number diagonally
♘ Knight	In an L-shape: 2 squares + 1 perpendicular; can jump over pieces
♙ Pawn	Forward 1 square; from starting position can move 2; captures diagonally forward
## Capturing
A piece captures an opponent's piece by moving onto its square.
Except for the knight, pieces cannot jump over other pieces.
Pawns capture differently from how they move.
## Check
A king is in check when an opponent's piece attacks it.
The player must immediately get out of check by:
Moving the king
Capturing the attacking piece
Blocking the attack (when possible)
## Checkmate
If the king is in check and there is no legal way to escape, it is checkmate → game over.
## Special Moves
Castling: King and rook move together under specific conditions.
En passant: A special pawn capture possible immediately after an opponent's pawn advances two squares.
Promotion: A pawn reaching the opposite end is promoted, usually to a queen, but it can become a rook, bishop, or knight.
## Illegal Moves
You cannot make a move that leaves your own king in check.
A king cannot move onto a square attacked by an opponent.
## Draws

A game can be drawn by:

Stalemate: Player has no legal move but is not in check.
Agreement between players.
Threefold repetition.
50-move rule.
Insufficient material to checkmate.
Other official draw conditions under tournament rules.
## Winning

You win by:

Checkmate, or
Your opponent resigning.

Basic principle: Protect your king, attack the opponent's king, and make only legal moves.
